01 · WHAT IT DOES

Your inbox, handled before you open it

Every email is classified, filed and summarised the moment it arrives. You read briefs, not walls of text.

Classify

Urgent, high, normal or low, plus the right folder. Fast rules first, on-device AI for the rest.

Summarise

A short brief for each thread: what it's about, who it's waiting on, the deadline and next steps.

Reply, your way

A suggested reply in the sender's language. Edit it, make it shorter or more formal, proofread it. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.

30-day clean-up

After you sign in, it sorts the last month and shows you what it will keep and discard. Nothing moves until you approve.

Quiet by default

Notifications only for urgent and high-priority mail. Everything else is filed silently.

Any language

Mail in other languages is shown in yours, and replies are drafted in theirs. Translation runs offline.

Discard, with a safety net

  • Marketing, newsletters and low-value notifications go to a Discarded folder, moved to Trash after 30 days.
  • Spam and phishing go to Junk.

Never discarded: people you've written to, your contacts, threads you're in, VIPs, security codes, and anything about money.

05 · PRIVATE BY DESIGN

Your email stays on your phone

The AI runs on the phone's own AI chip through Android's built-in Gemini Nano. There's no PEA server to send your mail to.

  • Processed on the device. Sorting, summaries, replies and translation all run locally.
  • The AI can't act on its own. It only returns a label, a summary or a draft. Instructions hidden inside emails are treated as text.
  • You send, always. No email leaves without your tap on Send.
  • Nothing deleted outright. Discarded mail can be restored for 30 days.
  • Credentials locked down. Passwords are encrypted with Android Keystore; Google and Microsoft use sign-in tokens.
06 · THE STORY

Why I built PEA

I work on the technical and product side of izi.TRAVEL, the audio-guide platform. My work inbox is where everything lands at once: helpdesk tickets from customers about payments and access, automated alerts from our payment providers, pull-request reviews for the website and the app, partner and sales mail, store and ad-network reports. It arrives in English, Russian and other languages. In the 30 days I measured, almost 1,900 emails reached my inbox. Most were useful to somebody; very few needed me; and those few were the easiest to miss.

First, an agent on my Mac

On 16 October 2026 I built a mail watcher with Claude Code. Every ten minutes it sorted new mail into action buckets with plain rules, asked an AI model which emails mattered, notified me, and opened a short brief of the thread with a reply I could edit and send in the sender's language. It worked, and it taught me two things. I read most of my mail on my phone, a Galaxy Z Fold7, not at my desk. And every brief meant sending customers' email to a cloud AI model, which I didn't want to do with other people's messages.

So: the same agent, on the phone, private

The Fold7 ships with Gemini Nano, Google's on-device model, so the AI can run on the phone's own chip with no server in between. I set a few rules for myself before writing any code: never lose an email, never send without my tap, keep a discarded email recoverable for 30 days, and make the AI optional, so the app is still useful on phones without it.

How it was built

The same day I wrote the design and a 55-task plan, each task small enough to finish and test on its own. Claude Code was the engineer and I was the product owner and tester: several AI agents worked in parallel on separate branches, and every change had to pass the same checks before it landed (unit tests, screenshot tests at every Fold7 screen size, lint and CI). I then ran it read-only against my real work mailbox: over 30 days of mail, the rules never discarded an email from someone I'd written to or a thread I'd replied in, and nothing in the mailbox was changed.

What the phone taught me

  • Gemini Nano only works while the app is on screen. So background sync sorts with rules, and the AI catches up when I open the app.
  • Android said the model was "still downloading" for days while it worked fine. PEA now tests the model with a tiny prompt instead of trusting the flag.
  • Translation with a dedicated on-device library made the app 74 MB. Gemini Nano translates instead, and the app is 6.6 MB.
  • Nano translated "Tuesday" into Russian as "Monday", every time. Weekdays are now spelled out for it and checked afterwards; a translation that changes a day isn't shown.
  • It's quick enough to use: about 3 seconds to sort an email, 5 to 8 for a thread summary and about 2 for a reply draft on the Fold7.

Where it is now

PEA is pre-alpha. I use it on my own work mail, and I'm sharing the build so others with the same problem can try it. Next comes measuring the AI on real mail, battery and speed, a security review and a signed beta.

Nishad Aliyar, technical and product at izi.TRAVEL. Built with Claude Code; published by Elephant Clock Technology.

08 · SUPPORTED DEVICES

Runs on Android 12 and newer

Sorting, the Priority inbox, alerts and replies work on any Android 12+ phone. How much AI you get depends on whether Google supports Gemini Nano on your model.

Full on-device AI

Gemini Nano with the Prompt API

  • Galaxy Z Fold7, Galaxy Z TriFold
  • Galaxy S26, S26+, S26 Ultra
  • Galaxy Z Flip8, Z Fold8, Z Fold8 Ultra

AI sorting, thread summaries, reply drafts, rewriting, proofreading and translation, including Russian and other non-English mail.

Rules only, for now

Google's smaller AI features only

  • Galaxy S25, S25+, S25 Ultra

These phones have Google's summarising, rewriting and proofreading models but not the general Gemini Nano model PEA builds on. Today they get the rules-only app; English summaries, rewriting and proofreading are planned.

Rules only

Every other Android 12+ phone

  • Older Galaxy S, Z Flip and Z Fold, Galaxy A, tablets
  • Pixel and other Android phones

The full app without AI. Sorting runs on rules that read every email's signals. The AI buttons stay hidden.

Device lists are Google's (ML Kit GenAI, updated 16 October 2026). Gemini Nano only runs while the app is on screen, so background sync always uses rules. Tested so far on a Galaxy Z Fold7.
